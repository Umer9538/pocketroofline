PocketRoofline · station 01 · iPhone 13 (A15 Bionic)

LLM inference measured on a phone, not an edge board.

RooflineBench characterised on-device inference across desktop GPUs, an M1 Pro, a Jetson and a Raspberry Pi — no phones. This extends the same roofline methodology to smartphone silicon, with every repeat retained, thermal state recorded, and the matrix re-run on every OS and runtime update. Thermal collapse on phones has been measured independently (arXiv:2603.23640, iPhone 16 Pro, −41.5% over 20 runs); what this adds is the roofline placement, a mains-powered control and the open per-build record.

Station 01 is the iPhone 13 whose figures follow. Also measured: station 02 · iPhone 15 Plus (A16 Bionic), three sessions, further down, with its caveats up front and each session shown on its own.

38%

Peak decode (42.8 tok/s, short prompts) overstates sustained decode (30.9 tok/s, after 5 consecutive long generations) by this much. Quoted phone-inference numbers are peak numbers.

Where this sits on the roofline

Single-stream decode reads every weight once per token, so decode throughput converts directly into achieved memory bandwidth (0.636 GB of weights per token). That places this workload firmly in the memory-bound region — which is why decode, not prefill, is what thermal throttling destroys.

Peak bandwidth here is a published vendor figure, not a measurement — and for the A15 the public figures disagree (Apple M1: 68.25 GB/s, consistently reported (128-bit LPDDR4X-4266) · A15 Bionic: public sources disagree: 34.1 GB/s (64-bit LPDDR4X-4266) vs 42.7 GB/s), so its utilisation is given as a range. A STREAM-style measured ceiling (METHODOLOGY §3) is owed and will replace these; until then, treat the achieved GB/s column as the real result and the percentage column as an indication. A measured ceiling now exists for the A16 Bionic at station 02, and its placement uses it.

Read the achieved column instead: the phone extracts a higher fraction of its memory system than the laptop does — it is simply working against a much smaller ceiling, and thermal throttling then takes away roughly a quarter of what it had.

PocketRoofline · station 02

iPhone 15 Plus (A16 Bionic, 6 GB)

Three benchmark sessions on this phone, each shown on its own and never pooled, oldest first: session 1, 2026-10-05 · iOS 27.0 (24A437) · warm start (fair) · live chart during repeats; session 2, 2026-11-08 · iOS 27.0.1 (24A446) · cold start · live chart during repeats; session 3, 2026-11-08 · iOS 27.0.1 (24A446) · cold start · still screen during repeats. Read the account below before using any figure from this phone.

The SILO step: what is known (applies to every session below)

  1. It recurs. SILO decode ends up far below LISO in all three sessions (two with the live chart, one with a still screen), on two OS builds (24A437, 24A446): 2026-10-05 (24A437, live): last LISO 59.17, first SILO 29.69, then 27.16–31.73 tok/s at serious; 2026-11-08 (24A446, live): last LISO 57.12, first SILO 42.01, then 24.94–25.78 tok/s at fair; 2026-11-08 (24A446, still): last LISO 55.88, first SILO 51.19, then 30.05–34.61 tok/s at serious.
  2. It happens with a still screen, so the live chart is not its main cause. After the step, SILO ran 30.05–34.61 tok/s with the screen still, against 27.16–31.73 (2026-10-05) and 24.94–25.78 (2026-11-08) with the live chart. That is one run of each kind, so the gap between them is not read as an effect of the chart.
  3. It arrives after roughly 70–100 s of continuous load, in every ordering, counting from the first measured repeat: in both cold benchmark sessions during the first SILO repeat, which ran from about 72 to 97 s; in the warm-start 2026-10-05 session already by the first SILO repeat, at about 72 s; in the SILO-first diagnostic at 81 s, where decode first fell 20% below its repeat's opening rate (measured). The benchmark times are estimated from the repeat timings, since those captures carry no clock.
  4. Decode bounces back after a pause. In the diagnostic, SILO repeat 4 sank to 42.0 tok/s in its last windows, and after the 3 s pause repeat 5 began at 57.7. The benchmark sessions carry no window trace, so whether they bounce back too cannot be seen; their repeat means stay low.
  5. The cause is still unexplained. A recovery test, which heats the phone and then times how long decode takes to come back, is the next experiment. Until the step is understood, no peak-to-sustained percentage is quoted for this phone and its SILO figures are not a sustained-throughput estimate. Station 01's harness drew nothing during a repeat; of these sessions, the still-screen one is the closest match to it.

What the diagnostic showed. One SILO-first run (2026-11-08, iOS 27.0.1 (24A446), cold start, still screen, network on): five 1024-token SILO repeats from a cool phone, with the decode rate of every 64-token window recorded.
